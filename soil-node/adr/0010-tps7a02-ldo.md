# ADR-0010 — TPS7A02 ultra-low-Iq LDO for the 3.3 V rail

**Status:** Accepted

## Context

The battery pack runs from 5.4 V fresh to ~3.4 V exhausted
([ADR-0009](0009-aa-lithium-battery.md)); the electronics need a stable 3.3 V.

The node is awake for about 2.5 seconds out of every 1800 — a duty cycle near
0.14 %. **The regulator is therefore powered 100 % of the time while the load it
serves is essentially absent 99.86 % of the time.** Its quiescent current is
consequently one of the largest single terms in the entire power budget, and
choosing it badly makes every other optimisation pointless.

## Background: what an LDO is

A **Low-DropOut regulator** takes a varying input voltage and holds a fixed
output. "Low dropout" means it needs only a small headroom — perhaps 3.4 V in
for 3.3 V out — unlike older linear regulators needing 1.5–2 V.

Mechanically it is a variable resistor in series with the load, adjusted by
feedback. The consequence worth internalising: **the excess voltage is burned as
heat, not converted.** At 4.5 V in for 3.3 V out, about 27 % of the energy is
wasted.

Two numbers characterise one for this application:

- **Quiescent current (Iq)** — what the regulator consumes simply being on.
- **Dropout voltage** — the minimum input-to-output difference for regulation,
  which sets how much of the battery is usable.

## Options considered

### A. TPS7A02 (chosen)

- **Pro:** **25 nA quiescent.** Effectively zero — under 0.2 % of the node's
  ~15 µA sleep budget. It disappears from the calculation entirely.
- **Pro:** ~100 mV dropout at light load, so regulation holds down to ~3.4 V,
  which extracts essentially the entire battery
  ([ADR-0009](0009-aa-lithium-battery.md)).
- **Pro:** 6 V maximum input accommodates the 5.4 V fresh pack.
- **Pro:** 200 mA output, comfortably above the ~50 mA peak during LoRa
  transmission.
- **Pro:** Tiny SOT-23-5 package, hand-solderable.
- **Con:** More expensive than a commodity regulator — a euro or two rather than
  cents.
- **Con:** Small output capacitor range and some sensitivity to capacitor ESR;
  follow the datasheet's recommendation rather than improvising.

### B. AMS1117-3.3 (the default on most dev boards)

- **Pro:** Costs cents, available everywhere, familiar.
- **Con:** **~5 mA quiescent current.** At 24 hours a day that is **120 mAh per
  day — roughly 85× the node's entire budget of 1.4 mAh/day.** A 3000 mAh pack
  would be flat in under a month.
- **Con:** ~1.1 V dropout, so it stops regulating at ~4.4 V and abandons most of
  the battery.
- **This is the single most common reason low-power projects fail.** The MCU is
  chosen for microamps and then a regulator quietly draws milliamps forever.
  Recorded here explicitly so the trap is never re-entered.

### C. HT7333 / MCP1700 (common "low power" LDOs)

- **Pro:** ~1.6 µA (MCP1700) or ~4 µA (HT7333) quiescent — a thousand-fold
  better than an AMS1117 and genuinely adequate.
- **Pro:** Cheap and widely available in through-hole packages.
- **Con:** Still 60–160× the TPS7A02, consuming 10–25 % of the sleep budget
  rather than 0.2 %. Since the part costs about a euro more, that is a poor
  trade on a device intended to run for years.
- A sound fallback if the TPS7A02 is unobtainable.

### D. Buck (switching) converter

- **Pro:** Converts rather than burns the excess voltage, recovering the ~27 %
  linear loss.
- **Con:** **Quiescent current of 10–50 µA even doing nothing**, which on a
  device sleeping 99.86 % of the time costs far more than the conversion loss
  saves. The efficiency advantage only materialises under continuous load, which
  this node never presents.
- **Con:** Switching noise on the rail during the sensitive ADC measurement, an
  inductor, more parts and more board area.
- The intuition that "switching is more efficient" is correct for continuous
  loads and wrong here. Worth recording, because it is a natural assumption.

### E. No regulator, run directly from the battery

- **Con:** 5.4 V from a fresh pack far exceeds the ESP32-C3's 3.6 V maximum.
  Not viable.

## Decision

**TPS7A0233PDBVR**, fixed 3.3 V output, EN tied to IN (permanently enabled —
25 nA costs nothing and switching it would require something else always awake).

## Consequences

- Regulator quiescent current effectively vanishes from the power budget. The
  ~15 µA sleep figure is then set by the ESP32-C3 (~5 µA), the ADS1115 (~2 µA)
  and board leakage.
- **Board leakage becomes the thing to watch.** With the regulator at 25 nA,
  flux residue, a stray pull-up or a forgotten LED matter. Clean the board after
  soldering, and include no indicator LEDs on the always-on rail.
- Input and output capacitors must follow the datasheet — 10 µF ceramic each,
  plus the 100 µF bulk capacitor to absorb the LoRa transmit current step.
- The linear efficiency loss is accepted: ~27 % of energy becomes heat. Against
  a 1.4 mAh/day load this is a rounding error, and the alternative costs more in
  quiescent current than it saves.
- The whole rail can never be switched off; the deep sleep current *is* the
  floor. This is fine, and it is why that floor was made as low as possible.
